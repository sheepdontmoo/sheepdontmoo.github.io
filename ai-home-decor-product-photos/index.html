AI home decor product photos that keep your real pattern, colour and size

Ask an AI for "a photo of a throw cushion ad" and you usually get someone else's cushion: a brighter colour, a neater pattern with bigger motifs, tassels you don't sell, a sofa that makes it look twice its size, and a "100% linen" badge nobody checked. On home decor that's more than off-brand. Buyers open the parcel, hold it up against the photo and put it on their own sofa. A colour that isn't real, a print that's been tidied up or a size that's been flattered turns into a return. These three free prompts start from your own photo and facts, so the pattern, the colour, the scale and the claims stay as they really are.

How to use them: 1) photograph your product by a window in daylight, straight on and flat, 2) upload it to ChatGPT and paste the prompt, 3) run the 6-point check below before you put anything in an ad or listing.

Use the attached photo as the exact reference for [a square 45 cm linen-blend throw cushion in rust orange with a cream hand-block-printed leaf pattern and a concealed zip on the bottom edge]. Keep the fabric colour, the printed pattern, the size of each motif, the spacing of the repeat, the direction of the print, the weave texture, the piping or edge finish and any small irregularities in the hand print exactly as they are in the photo; do not recolour, sharpen, simplify, mirror, enlarge or tidy the pattern, do not make the print look machine-perfect, and do not add tassels, piping, buttons, labels or a logo that aren't in the photo. Tip: Your customer chose this cushion for the print. If the leaves in the ad are bigger, brighter or neater than the ones that arrive, she'll feel it the moment she opens the parcel. Tip: "Smaller than I expected" is the review that sinks cushion listings, because 45 cm sounds big and looks small on a deep sofa. Put your real measurements in the brackets and say how you measured (seam to seam or with the insert). Then put an A4 notebook against your real cushion and check the ratio; if the AI has made it bigger, trust your tape measure, not the render.

Listing copy with no made-up fabric or care claims

Paste your real product facts and your supplier's fabric spec into the brackets. ChatGPT · any ChatGPT model, paste your real product facts and your supplier's fabric spec first

You are a careful ecommerce copywriter for a small home and living brand. Here are my product's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. product name, size and how I measured it, fabric composition from my supplier's spec sheet, how the print is made, cover only or insert included and the insert's fill and size, closure type, colour name and how I photographed it, care instructions from the label, any certificate I actually hold and its number, where it's made, what's in the box, price, delivery and returns]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof before I say it (for example 100% linen, organic, hand-printed, eco-friendly, sustainable, machine washable, fade-resistant, made in a named country, certified), or don't say it at all. Then, using only the safe facts, write: a 60-word product description; 5 bullet points that each turn one fact into one everyday benefit at home; 6 short ad hooks (under 12 words each); and short answers to the three questions shoppers ask most before buying a cushion online (what size is it really, is the insert included, will the colour look like the photo). Rules: never say luxury, best, #1, premium quality, eco or sustainable unless my facts prove it; do not invent materials, percentages, origins, certifications, reviews, star ratings or what customers said; if a line needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on.

Tip: Your listing is the promise the parcel has to keep. Check every line against the fact it lists, copy fabric percentages from your supplier's spec rather than memory, and say "cover only" or "insert included" in the first bullet so nobody has to message you to find out.

The 6-point home decor accuracy check

  1. Colour: hold your real product next to the screen by a window. AI loves to warm up neutrals and brighten rusts, greens and blues. If it drifts, re-run with "exact same muted colour as the photo" or use your own photo.
  2. Pattern: count a few motifs across the front and compare them with your real product. AI enlarges, mirrors and tidies prints, and makes hand-printed or handmade work look machine-perfect.
  3. Size: judge it against something everyone knows, like an A4 notebook, a mug or a standard sofa seat. Say how you measured (seam to seam, with or without the insert).
  4. What's in the box: only show what actually ships. If it's a cover only, say so in the picture and in the first line of the caption.
  5. Materials and care: delete any "100% linen", "organic", "hand-woven", "machine washable" or "eco" wording the AI wrote. Use only what your supplier's spec sheet and care label say.
  6. Badges and logos: remove any certification mark, award, tag or brand logo the AI added that your product and paperwork don't back up.

If a render fails any of these, re-run it with the fix spelled out ("same number of leaves across the front as the photo", "true size, 45 by 45 cm, next to an A4 notebook") or use your own photo for that shot. A real phone photo beats an inaccurate AI one.
