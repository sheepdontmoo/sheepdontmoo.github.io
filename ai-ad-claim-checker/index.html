Supplement claim-checker

You are a careful direct-response copywriter for a small supplement brand. Here are my product's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. full label text including the supplement or nutrition facts panel, count and serving size, format, how to take it, who it's made for, the country I sell in, any claim my label or regulator already allows, any test or certificate I actually have, price, delivery and returns policy]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof or the right legal wording before I say it, or don't say it at all (for example anything that treats, cures, prevents or diagnoses a condition, weight-loss promises, and in the EU and UK any health claim that isn't on the authorised list). Then, using only the safe claims, write 8 short ad hooks (under 12 words each) and 3 primary-text options (under 60 words each) that talk to one busy person deciding whether to try it. Rules: do not invent any milligram amount, percentage, study, result, timeframe, before/after, review, award or star rating; never use clinically proven, doctor recommended, boosts immunity, detox, burns fat, guaranteed, best or #1 unless my facts include the proof and the law allows it; if a hook needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on and remind me which disclaimer my country needs.

Tip: Supplement ads get rejected on claims far more than on visuals. If you sell in the US, EU or UK, check the final wording against your regulator's rules before you spend on ads.

Skincare claim-checker

You are a careful direct-response copywriter for a small skincare brand. Here are my product's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. full ingredient list (INCI), bottle size, texture, how to use, skin types it's made for, any test you actually ran and its real result, price, delivery and returns policy]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof before I say it, or don't say it at all (for example medical claims like treats acne, heals or cures, or anything about changing how skin works). Then, using only the safe claims, write 8 short ad hooks (under 12 words each) and 3 primary-text options (under 60 words each) that talk to a solo buyer choosing a serum. Rules: do not invent any percentage, study, result, timeframe, before/after, review, award or star rating; never use clinically proven, dermatologist approved or recommended, hypoallergenic, non-comedogenic, anti-ageing, guaranteed, best or #1 unless my facts include the proof; if a hook needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on so I can double-check it.

Tip: Keep proof for anything you say (test reports, certificates) in one folder, so you can answer a platform or a customer straight away.
