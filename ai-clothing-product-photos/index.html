AI clothing product photos that keep your real garment accurate

Ask an AI for "a photo of a linen shirt ad" and you usually get someone else's shirt: a different colour, extra buttons, a made-up brand and a model wearing it tighter than it really fits. That looks fine until the parcel arrives and the customer sends it back. These three free prompts start from your own photos and facts, so the colour, details and fit stay as they really are.

How to use them: 1) take your photos by a window in daylight, 2) upload them to ChatGPT and paste the prompt, 3) run the 6-point check below before you put anything in an ad or listing.

True-colour product shot from your phone photo

Use the attached photo as the exact garment reference. Keep the colour, fabric texture, weave, print, buttons, stitching, collar, pockets, labels and proportions exactly as they are; do not recolour, brighten, saturate, smooth the fabric or add anything. Correct only the lighting: the garment laid flat and centred on a plain neutral light-grey surface, soft even daylight-balanced light with a neutral white balance (no warm or cool cast), a very faint natural shadow at the edges, true-to-life colour that matches the garment in real daylight, its natural creases kept, sharp focus across the fabric. Photorealistic ecommerce product shot. Vertical 4:5.

Tip: "The colour looked different online" is a common reason clothes come back. Take your reference photo by a window in daylight, then hold the real garment next to the screen; if the render is warmer, cooler or brighter than the real thing, add "match the reference colour exactly, do not brighten" and re-run. Name the colour in your listing the way it really looks (oatmeal, not white).

Front and back from your own two photos

Use the two attached photos as the exact reference for the front and the back of the same garment. Keep the colour, fabric, buttons, collar, yoke, pleats, seams, any back print or label and the proportions exactly as they are in each photo; do not invent any detail on the back that isn't in the back photo, and do not copy front details onto the back. Create one clean image showing the garment twice, side by side at the same scale: front on the left, back on the right, each laid flat and symmetrical with the sleeves relaxed, on a seamless warm off-white background, soft even studio light, faint natural shadows, true-to-life colour, sharp focus, photorealistic ecommerce product shot, no text, no labels added. Square 1:1.

Tip: Give the AI only a front photo and it will make up the back. Always attach a real back photo, then check the yoke, seams, length and any print against your garment before you use the image.

Fit-and-fabric copy that only says what's true

Paste your real measurements and care-label facts into the brackets. ChatGPT · any ChatGPT model, paste your real measurements and label facts first

You are a careful ecommerce copywriter for a small clothing brand. Here are my garment's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. garment name, fibre content from the care label, fabric weight if you know it, fit (relaxed, regular or slim), the measurements for every size I stock (chest, length, sleeve), the height and size of anyone who modelled it, how it washes and whether it shrinks, where it's made, any certificate I actually hold, price, delivery and returns policy]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof before I say it (for example organic, recycled, sustainable, ethically made, pre-shrunk, wrinkle-free), or don't say it at all. Then, using only the safe facts, write: a 60-word product description; a fit and sizing note that tells someone between two sizes which to pick, based only on my measurements; 6 short ad hooks (under 12 words each) about fit, fabric or feel; and short answers to the three questions shoppers ask most before buying clothes (will it fit me, is it see-through, how does it wash). Rules: never say true to size, one size fits all, flattering on every body, sustainable, eco-friendly, premium quality, best or #1 unless my facts prove it; do not invent measurements, reviews, return rates, star ratings or what customers said; if a line needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on.

Tip: Fit and size are among the most common reasons people send clothes back, and green claims like "sustainable" are getting closer scrutiny from regulators in the EU, UK and US. Check every line against the fact it lists, and keep your real measurements on the product page so shoppers can check the size advice themselves.

The 6-point garment accuracy check

  1. Colour: hold the real garment next to the screen in daylight. Warmer, cooler or brighter than the real thing means re-run, not ship.
  2. Hardware and trims: count the buttons, check the zip, drawstrings, pockets and stitching. AI quietly adds or drops them.
  3. The back: if you didn't upload a back photo, the AI invented it. Don't use an invented back.
  4. Labels and text: care labels, size tags and printed logos come out garbled. Compare letter for letter, or use a real photo.
  5. Fit: the AI tends to slim, lengthen or tighten clothes. If the fit looks better than it does on a real body, it will cost you returns.
  6. Sheerness and texture: make sure the fabric looks as thick, sheer, shiny or matte as it really is.

If a render fails any of these, re-run it with the fix spelled out ("match the reference colour exactly", "keep exactly five buttons") or use your own photo for that shot. A real phone photo beats an inaccurate AI one.
