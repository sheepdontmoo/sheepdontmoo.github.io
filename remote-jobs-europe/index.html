Remote work in Europe, in numbers

  • 8.9% of employed people aged 20-64 in the EU usually worked from home in 2025 (5.5% in 2019); a further 14.4% sometimes did. Ireland: 19.8% usually. (Source: Eurostat, lfsa_ehomp (employed persons working from home), 2025 data, updated 10 Sep 2026)
  • Share of Indeed postings mentioning remote/hybrid work, 30 Sep 2026: Ireland 16.8%, UK 15.9%, Germany 13.9%, Canada 13.8%, Australia 12.1%, France 12.1%, US 8.6%. (Source: Indeed Hiring Lab Remote Tracker, remote_postings.csv, 30 Sep 2026)

Remote work has grown across the EU, but "usually from home" is still under 1 in 10 workers. Fully remote roles are a minority, which is why a checked shortlist saves time.

"EU remote" usually means one country

Most "remote in Europe" listings are really "remote in these countries", because the employer needs a local entity or employer-of-record to put you on payroll. In the sample above, EU roles list: Czechia, France, Italy, Poland. The club's EU tag means at least one EU country is listed; always read the exact country list.

  • EU citizens generally don't need a work permit to work anywhere in the EU (Your Europe: work permits). Whether a remote employer can hire you in your country is a payroll question, not a permit question.
  • Non-EU citizens usually need the right to work in the listed country. Check before you apply.
  • "EMEA" often includes the UK, Middle East and Africa too. Ask which countries they can hire in.
  • Contractor roles may pay you as a freelancer, so you handle your own tax and social insurance.

Language and time zone

Many EU remote roles are in English, but sales and support roles often need the local language: a role listed for one country often means its language too. Time zones are easier: most EU roles work on CET, within one or two hours of you if you're in Europe.

Scam signs to watch

Be wary of "remote jobs in Europe" adverts on social media that promise visa sponsorship or high pay for simple work and then ask for a fee. Never pay to get a job, never interview only by chat app, and never move money for an "employer". Our remote job scam checklist takes three minutes.

FAQ

Can I work remotely for any EU company if I'm an EU citizen?

You generally don't need a work permit anywhere in the EU, but each employer decides which countries it can hire and pay in. Check the listed countries in the job ad.

What does the EU tag mean?

The listing says it hires in at least one EU country. Read the exact country list in the listing before you apply.

Do I need to speak another language?

Many tech and operations roles are in English. Sales and customer roles often need the local language. The listing will say.

Are these jobs free to apply for?

Yes. You apply on the employer's or job board's page. Never pay for a job, visa or training.
