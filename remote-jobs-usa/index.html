What "Remote, US" usually means

  1. You live in the US. Most US-only listings need you on US payroll, so "remote" rarely means "from abroad".
  2. Sometimes only certain states. Many US remote listings name the states they can hire in, because payroll and tax are set up state by state. Check the state list before you apply.
  3. You can prove you're authorised to work. Every US employer uses Form I-9 to verify the identity and employment authorization of people it hires (USCIS: Form I-9). A real employer does this after an offer, through a proper process, not by text before an interview.

FAQ

Can I work a US remote job from outside the US?

Usually not. US-tagged listings normally need you to live in the US and be authorised to work there. Look for Anywhere-tagged roles if you're abroad.

Why do some US remote jobs list only certain states?

Employers set up payroll and tax state by state, so many only hire in the states where they're registered. The listing will say which states.

Are these US remote jobs free to apply for?

Yes. You apply on the employer's or job board's page. A real employer never charges you to apply, for equipment or for a background check.

Where do I report a US job scam?

Report it to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and the FBI's IC3 at ic3.gov. If you've paid or shared bank details, call your bank first.
