AI food and drink product photos that keep your real label accurate

Ask an AI for "a photo of a yuzu soda ad" and you usually get someone else's drink: a made-up brand, a glowing colour your drink doesn't have, and a "100% natural" badge you never earned. On food and drink that isn't just off-brand. Wrong numbers, missing allergens or health claims you can't prove can get a listing pulled. These three free prompts start from your own photos and facts, so the label, the count and the claims stay as they really are.

How to use them: 1) take your photos by a window in daylight, 2) upload them to ChatGPT and paste the prompt, 3) run the 6-point check below before you put anything in an ad or listing.

Listing copy with no made-up health claims

Paste your real label facts into the brackets. ChatGPT · any ChatGPT model, paste your real label facts first

You are a careful ecommerce copywriter for a small food and drink brand. Here are my product's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. product name, pack size, full ingredients list and allergens from the label, nutrition per serving, how it's made, where it's made, shelf life and storage, any certificate I actually hold (organic, vegan, gluten-free), price, delivery and returns]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof before I say it (for example natural, organic, healthy, low sugar, high protein, gut-friendly, vegan, gluten-free, locally sourced), or don't say it at all. Then, using only the safe facts, write: a 60-word product description; a taste note that describes flavour honestly without promising health benefits; 6 short ad hooks (under 12 words each) about taste, occasion or ingredients; and short answers to the three questions shoppers ask most before buying food and drink online (what's in it, how much do I get, how long does it keep). Rules: never say healthy, guilt-free, detox, boosts immunity, good for your gut, clean, best or #1 unless my facts prove it; do not invent nutrition figures, reviews, star ratings or what customers said; if a line needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on.

Tip: Nutrition and health claims on food are tightly regulated in the EU, UK and US, and words like "low sugar" or "high protein" have legal definitions. Check every line against the fact it lists, and keep your full ingredients and allergens on the product page.

The 6-point label accuracy check

  1. Numbers: compare every nutrition figure, percentage and serving size with your real label. AI rounds, swaps and invents digits.
  2. Allergens: check the allergen line word for word. A garbled allergen statement is a safety problem, not a style problem.
  3. Size: judge the pack against something everyone knows, like a standard glass or a hand. AI often makes products bigger.
  4. Count: count cans, bars or sachets in a multipack shot. If the box holds 12, the picture must show 12.
  5. Colour: pour or plate the real thing by a window and compare. AI makes drinks and food brighter and glossier than they are.
  6. Claims and badges: delete any "natural", "healthy", "zero sugar", award or certification mark the AI added that your label and certificates don't back up.

If a render fails any of these, re-run it with the fix spelled out ("keep every number exactly as in the photo", "show exactly 12 cans") or use your own photo for that shot. A real phone photo beats an inaccurate AI one.
