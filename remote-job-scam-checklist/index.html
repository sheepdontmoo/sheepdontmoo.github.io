Remote job scam checklist: 3 rules and 10 red flags

The quick way to check a remote job before you apply, share ID or reply to a "recruiter". It takes about three minutes.

Why it matters

  • Employment-fraud losses reported to IC3: $70.2M (2023), $264.2M (2024), $362.9M (2025); 24,688 complaints in 2025. (Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), 2025 Internet Crime Report)
  • Median loss to task scams in BBB Scam Tracker data hit $2,300 in 2025; nearly 50,000 people reported falling victim to an employment scam to BBB over the last three years. (Source: BBB Scam Studies, 'Employment scams 2026 update')
  • In 2025, victims reported losses of almost $13 million to AI-involved employment scams; IC3 describes voice spoofing / possible deepfakes in online interviews. (Source: FBI IC3, 2025 Internet Crime Report (AI section))

The 3 rules that stop almost every scam

  1. Never pay to get a job. No "training fee", "equipment deposit", "starter kit", "background check fee" or "visa fee". Real employers pay you, not the other way round.
  2. Never interview only by chat app. A "recruiter" who moves you to Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal or Google Chat for a text-only "interview" is the classic scam.
  3. Never deposit a cheque or move money for an employer. "We'll send you a cheque to buy your equipment, then you send the rest to our vendor" is fake-cheque fraud. You lose the money when the cheque bounces.

10 red flags (one is a warning, two is a no)

  1. A job offer without a real interview, or "you've been selected" for a job you never applied to.
  2. Pay far above normal for the work ("$45/hr data entry, no experience").
  3. Recruiter emails from Gmail/Outlook instead of the company domain, or a lookalike domain (amaz0n-careers.com).
  4. A vague company with no website, no LinkedIn page, or a website created last month.
  5. Pressure: "reply in 2 hours or the role goes to someone else".
  6. Asking for bank details, passport or national ID before a written offer.
  7. "Reshipping", "package inspector", "payment processing agent" or "mystery shopper" roles.
  8. Network marketing / MLM: you earn by recruiting others or buying stock. "Be your own boss", "financial freedom".
  9. Commission-only "sales" with no base pay, presented as a normal job.
  10. Task or "app optimisation" jobs where you top up a balance to unlock tasks (task scams).

How to check a job in 3 minutes

  1. Find the job on the company's own careers page. Search "[company] careers" and look for the same role. If it only exists in a chat message, walk away.
  2. Check the apply link's domain. It should be the company's domain or a known hiring system such as greenhouse.io, lever.co, ashbyhq.com, workable.com, smartrecruiters.com, myworkdayjobs.com or teamtailor.com.
  3. Check the recruiter. Real recruiters have a LinkedIn profile that lists the company, and email from the company domain.
  4. Check the video call. If the voice and lip movement don't match, or the "interviewer" won't turn the camera on, pause and verify the company another way.

If you think you've been scammed

  • Stop contact. Don't send more money or documents.
  • Call your bank straight away if you paid or shared card details.
  • Report it. UK: Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud; in Scotland, Police Scotland on 101). US: FTC and the FBI's IC3. Ireland: your local Garda station. Elsewhere: your national police fraud unit.

FAQ

Do real remote jobs ask for a fee?

No. Training, equipment, starter-kit or background-check fees are a scam sign. Real employers pay you.

Is a Telegram or WhatsApp interview legit?

Text-only interviews on chat apps are one of the most common recruitment scam patterns. Find the role on the company's own careers page first.

What is a task scam?

A 'job' that pays you to rate products or boost apps, then asks you to deposit money to unlock tasks or withdraw earnings. Real jobs never ask you to pay in.

Where do I report a job scam?

UK: Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk). US: the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov) and the FBI's IC3 (ic3.gov). Ireland: your local Garda station. Contact your bank first if you've paid.
