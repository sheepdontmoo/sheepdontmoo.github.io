AI tech and gadget product photos that keep your real device accurate

Ask an AI for "a photo of a wireless speaker ad" and you usually get someone else's gadget: a made-up brand, a glowing screen showing an app your product doesn't have, an extra port, and a "40H playtime" badge nobody measured. On gadgets that's more than off-brand. Buyers plug it in, switch it on and check. A wrong port, a fake screen or an invented spec turns into a return. These three free prompts start from your own photos and facts, so the screen, the ports and the claims stay as they really are.

How to use them: 1) take your photos by a window in daylight and grab a real screenshot of the display, 2) upload them to ChatGPT and paste the prompt, 3) run the 6-point check below before you put anything in an ad or listing.

Your real screen, from your own screenshot

Upload a photo of your product and a screenshot (or straight-on photo) of its real display, then paste this into ChatGPT. GPT Image (ChatGPT) · attach 2 images (product photo + real screen capture or straight-on photo of the lit display), 4:5

Use the first attached photo as the exact reference for the product: [a compact matte-black portable wireless speaker with a small square display on the front and one USB-C port on the back]. Keep its shape, colour, buttons, grille, port and any logo exactly as they are. Use the second attached image as the exact content of the product's display: copy it onto the display as it is, keeping every icon, word, number, layout and colour unchanged; do not redraw, tidy, translate, add or remove anything on the screen, and do not invent battery levels, track names, app screens, notifications or features. Change only the setting: the product standing on a pale oak desk, the display facing the camera straight on with no glare across it, soft even daylight from a window on the left, a plain warm white wall softly out of focus behind, true-to-life colour, sharp focus on the display, photorealistic, no text outside the display. Vertical 4:5.

Tip: Your customer will turn the product on and look at that screen. If the ad shows a screen it can't actually show, that's the first thing she'll notice. Zoom in and compare every icon and number with your real screenshot; AI still bends small screen text, so if anything has changed, put your real screenshot onto the render in Canva instead.

Your real cable in your real port

Use the attached photo as the exact reference for [a compact matte-black portable wireless speaker with a small square display on the front and one USB-C port on the back] and its cable. Keep the port, its shape, position and number exactly as in the photo, and keep the cable, its connector, colour, length and any markings exactly as they are; do not add ports, adapters, extra cables, charging icons, lights or labels that aren't in the photo, and do not change the connector type. Change only the setting and light: a close three-quarter view of the back of the product with the cable plugged in, resting on a plain matte light-grey surface, soft directional light from the upper left raking across the casing to show its real texture, a soft natural shadow, true-to-life colour, sharp focus on the port and connector, shallow depth of field, photorealistic, no text. Square 1:1.

Tip: "Will my charger fit?" is one of the most common questions buyers ask about gadgets, and a wrong port in an ad is one of the quickest ways to a return. Photograph the cable you actually ship, check the connector in the render against it, and write the port type in plain words in your caption.

Listing copy with no made-up specs

Paste your real spec sheet and your own test notes into the brackets. ChatGPT · any ChatGPT model, paste your real spec sheet and your own test notes first

You are a careful ecommerce copywriter for a small gadget brand. Here are my product's real facts: [paste your exact facts, e.g. product name, size and weight, ports and cable included, connection type and version, battery capacity from the manufacturer's sheet, playtime and charge time from my own tests and how I tested them, water or dust rating and whether I hold the test certificate, which devices it works with and which it doesn't, what's in the box, warranty, price, delivery and returns]. First, list every claim in my facts and sort it into: safe to say as written, needs proof before I say it (for example battery hours, fast charging, waterproof, drop-proof, range, compatible with all phones, certified), or don't say it at all. Then, using only the safe facts, write: a 60-word product description; 5 bullet points that each turn one fact into one everyday benefit; 6 short ad hooks (under 12 words each); and short answers to the three questions shoppers ask most before buying a gadget online (will it work with my phone, how long does the battery really last, what's in the box). Rules: never say best, fastest, #1, military-grade, all-day or waterproof unless my facts prove it; do not invent numbers, test results, certifications, reviews, star ratings or what customers said; if a line needs a fact I didn't give you, skip it and tell me which fact you'd need. After the copy, list which fact each line relies on.

Tip: A spec in your ad is a promise, and buyers hold you to it. Check every line against the fact it lists, keep "tested by us" figures labelled as your own tests, and put the full spec sheet and what's in the box on the product page so nobody has to guess.

The 6-point gadget accuracy check

  1. Screen: compare every icon, word and number on the display with your real screenshot. AI redraws interfaces and invents battery levels and notifications. If it drifts, put your real screenshot on in Canva, or show the screen switched off.
  2. Ports: count the ports and check the connector type against your real product. A port that isn't there is a refund waiting to happen.
  3. Cable and box: only show cables, chargers and adapters that actually ship in the box. If the wall plug isn't included, say so.
  4. Size: judge the device against something everyone knows, like a bank card or a hand. AI often makes gadgets bigger or slimmer than they are.
  5. Specs and figures: delete any battery hours, wattage, range or capacity the AI wrote. Put only figures from your spec sheet or your own timed tests in the caption, and say how you tested.
  6. Badges and logos: remove any "waterproof", certification mark, award or real brand logo the AI added that your certificates and product don't back up.

If a render fails any of these, re-run it with the fix spelled out ("keep the display exactly as in the screenshot", "one USB-C port only, on the back") or use your own photo for that shot. A real phone photo beats an inaccurate AI one.
