"Remote, UK" usually means three things

  1. You live in the UK. Most employers need you on UK payroll for tax and employment law, so "remote" rarely means "from Spain for the winter". Ask before you assume.
  2. You have the right to work. UK employers must check every applicant's right to work before they employ them (GOV.UK: check a job applicant's right to work). A real employer asks for this after an offer, through a proper process, not by WhatsApp before an interview.
  3. Occasional travel may still apply. Some "remote" roles want you at an office a few times a year. The club drops roles that ask for regular office days.

Where UK remote roles tend to be

The mix changes daily. Customer support, operations, people/HR, finance and software roles show up most often across drops; commission-only "sales" adverts are filtered out.

UK-specific scam signs

  • A "recruiter" asks you to pay for a DBS check or training before an offer. Real employers arrange and pay for checks.
  • You're asked to receive parcels or money and forward them. That can make you part of fraud or money laundering.
  • Interview by text only, on Telegram or WhatsApp.

Report recruitment scams in England, Wales and Northern Ireland to Report Fraud (it replaced Action Fraud); in Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101. Forward scam emails to report@phishing.gov.uk (NCSC).

FAQ

Can I work remotely for a UK company from abroad?

Sometimes, but most UK-only listings need you to live in the UK for payroll and employment law. If a listing says UK, assume you must be UK-based unless the employer says otherwise.

Are UK remote jobs on this page free to apply for?

Yes. You apply on the employer's or job board's page. A genuine UK employer never charges you to apply or for a DBS or right-to-work check.

What does the UK tag mean?

The listing says you need to be based in the UK. Anywhere-tagged roles may also accept UK applicants; check the time zone.

How do I report a UK job scam?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, report it to Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk), which replaced Action Fraud. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101.
