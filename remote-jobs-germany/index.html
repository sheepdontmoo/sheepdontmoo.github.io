No statutory right to home office in Germany

Unlike Ireland's right to request remote working, Germany still has no general statutory right to home office or mobile work. Employees can ask, but employers are not required to agree, reply by a deadline, or give reasons. Any arrangement usually comes from the employment contract, a works agreement or a collective agreement. The Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs summarises this on its Homeoffice page.

So if remote work matters to you, it is still faster to apply for roles that are remote from day one than to negotiate home office after you start.

What Germany-based applicants should check

  1. EU tags that list Germany. An EU-tagged lead means at least one EU country is listed. Confirm Germany (or "DACH" / DE) is in the list before you apply.
  2. Other EU-country tags. A role listed only for Poland, Hungary or Bulgaria is not a Germany role. You generally need the right to work and be hired in the listed country.
  3. Anywhere tags. These employers hire from more than one country. Confirm Germany is allowed and whether you will be an employee or a contractor.
  4. US / UK tags usually need you there. Do not apply from Germany unless the listing explicitly includes Germany.
  5. Language and hours. Many tech roles are in English; sales and support often need German. Most EU roles work on CET, which matches Germany.
  6. EU citizens generally don't need a work permit elsewhere in the EU (Your Europe: work permits); payroll setup in Germany is still the employer's decision.

Germany job-scam signs

The same patterns show up in Germany: fees to "get hired", text-only interviews on Telegram or WhatsApp, fake equipment payments, and task jobs that ask you to top up a balance. Median loss to task scams in BBB Scam Tracker data hit $2,300 in 2025; nearly 50,000 people reported falling victim to an employment scam to BBB over the last three years. (Source: BBB Scam Studies, 'Employment scams 2026 update') Report fraud to the police via your local station or the official Onlinewache portal (for internet fraud, pick the Bundesland where you live). See also the BKA guidance on filing a complaint, and our remote job scam checklist.

FAQ

Can I get a fully remote job in Germany?

Yes. Some employers hire fully remote staff in Germany, other EU-country roles may accept you if Germany is listed, and Anywhere roles accept applicants from more than one country. Always check the country list.

Do I have a legal right to home office in Germany?

No general statutory right. You can ask, but the employer does not have to agree. Arrangements usually come from contract, works agreement or collective agreement (see BMAS Homeoffice page).

Does your club have a Germany region tag?

Not yet. Drops use US, UK, EU and Anywhere. Germany-based members should prefer EU leads that list Germany, then other EU leads, then Anywhere.

Are these Germany remote jobs free to apply for?

Yes. You apply on the employer's or job board's page. A real employer never charges you to apply or start.

Where do I report a job scam in Germany?

Report it to the police via your local station or portal.onlinewache.polizei.de. If you paid or shared banking details, contact your bank first.
